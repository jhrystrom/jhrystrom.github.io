Method & sources

Required now = goal ÷ Π(1 − rᵢ) over each year between your current and target age, where rᵢ is the annual decline rate for that age and sex. Per-decade VO₂max rates are converted to annual rates by r = 1 − (1 − d)1/10. Anchors come from the studies below; values between anchors are interpolations and should be read as an estimate, not a prescription. Fleg et al. found the accelerating decline occurred regardless of activity habits — fitter people stay ahead because they start higher, which is exactly the point of training now.

Cardio: activity cost = METs × 3.5 ml/kg/min; required VO₂max = cost ÷ sustainable fraction (an activity you want to keep up for more than a few minutes should sit well below your max).

  1. Fleg JL et al. Accelerated longitudinal decline of aerobic capacity in healthy older adults. Circulation 2005;112:674–682. (Peak VO₂ decline rises from ~3–6%/decade in the 20s–30s to >20%/decade after 70; 8.3%/decade in 40-year-old men, 23.2%/decade in 70-year-old men; steeper in men than women.)
  2. Goodpaster BH et al. The loss of skeletal muscle strength, mass, and quality in older adults: the Health ABC Study. J Gerontol A 2006;61:1059–1064. (Knee-extensor strength loss in 70s: ~3.4%/yr men, ~2.6%/yr women.)
  3. Lindle RS et al. Age and gender comparisons of muscle strength in 654 women and men aged 20–93 yr. J Appl Physiol 1997;83:1581–1587. (Strength loss begins in midlife and accelerates with age; used for pre-70 shape.)
  4. Ainsworth BE et al. Compendium of Physical Activities. Med Sci Sports Exerc 2011;43:1575–1581 (and the 2024 update). (MET values for presets.)
  5. Mandsager K et al. Association of cardiorespiratory fitness with long-term mortality. JAMA Netw Open 2018;1:e183605. (Cited in Outlive for the importance of VO₂max.)
